\subsection{Experimental Design and Protocol}
\label{sec:exp-protocol}

We structured the evaluation program into four complementary tracks, documented in the project experiment logs (\path{docs/experimentos/}, \path{backend/experiments/results/}).

\textbf{Track~A — Operational pipeline (18~months).} Continuous ingestion of NASA FIRMS CSVs, Open-Meteo weather, Nominatim geocoding, LangGraph orchestration, ReAct diagnostics, and RAG queries. Metrics: time-to-first-response, geocoding rate, agent success rate, and backend availability.

\textbf{Track~B — Municipal next-day prediction (TASK-083, v3--v9).} Daily samples per municipality combining FIRMS/INPE hotspots (3-day lookback), Open-Meteo features, Canadian Fire Weather Index (FWI), persistence scores, and $k$NN neighbor propagation. Temporal split 70/10/20\%. Models: MLP, LSTM, XGBoost, Koopman-Det, NeKo-PIGNN. Primary metrics: RMSE, R\textsuperscript{2}, and three-class alert precision on the YES class.

\textbf{Track~C — GOES-16 unsupervised benchmark.} Validation against INPE BDQueimadas on 2024-10-31 (channels 7, 13, 14; hours 16--18~UTC; $72\times72$ grid). Methods: digital twin assimilation, isolation forest, spatial residual, \emph{combined persistence} (peak + temporal persistence + fusion), and \emph{consensus ensemble} (2-of-3 vote across twin, persistence, and residual views) as proposed in \path{docs/METODOLOGIA_NOVA_PROPOSTA.md}.

\textbf{Track~D — Agent and RAG evaluation.} 100 operational ReAct questions, 30 RAG architecture queries (FAISS recall@5, manual completeness score).

Reproducibility commands are archived in \path{docs/DOCUMENTACAO_PESQUISA_E_TESTES.md}. Example:

\subsection{GOES-16 Unsupervised Detection Benchmark}
\label{sec:goes16-validation}

GOES-16 ABI CMIPF was evaluated against INPE ground references for 2024-10-31 (76 INPE hotspots; 5{,}184 valid grid cells; 284 truth cells after $3\times3$ morphological dilation). Table~\ref{tab:goes-validation} reports five unsupervised methods. Low F1 ($\leq 0.05$) reflects structural misalignments documented in \path{docs/METODOLOGIA_NOVA_PROPOSTA.md}: temporal window mismatch, thermal anomaly vs.\ active-fire semantics, 2~km GOES cells vs.\ point-like INPE records, and CMIPF brightness temperature vs.\ dedicated AF products.

\begin{table}[htbp]
\centering

\label{tab:goes-validation}
\small
\begin{tabular}{@{}lrrrrrr@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Method} & \textbf{TP} & \textbf{FP} & \textbf{FN} & \textbf{P} & \textbf{R} & \textbf{F1} \\

The digital twin multi-band method achieved the best F1 (0.049) while maintaining 77.7\% overall accuracy. The \emph{combined persistence} and \emph{consensus ensemble} lines (Lines~B and~E in the methodology proposal) prioritize precision over recall; on this single-day grid benchmark they yield zero detections but also zero false positives --- suitable for high-stakes alert filtering when fused with FIRMS in the operational pipeline.

\subsection{Three-Level Operational Alert System}
\label{sec:three-level}

Following TASK-083 v8/v9 (\path{backend/experiments/results/TASK-083_FINAL.md}), we operationalize predictions as three response levels rather than binary fire/no-fire decisions (Table~\ref{tab:alert-levels}). Binary classification on the same data peaked at 47\% precision (v7) with 270 false positives (v3), illustrating the rare-event trade-off documented in \path{docs/EXPLICACAO_SISTEMA_DETECCAO.md}.
